Privacy policy

This page explains how data is processed by the WasteNot Android app, the API at waste-not-api.bccdev.id, and the public WasteNot pages. The maintainer is Oktavianus Samuel Minarto. You can send questions and data requests to oktaavsm@student.ub.ac.id.

The logs and attachments you save are linked to your account. A scan photo is sent when you run a scan, not when you simply open the camera. Scan photos and log attachments are stored in different ways.

Leave faces, identity documents, addresses, and other sensitive details out of your photos and notes unless you really need them.

Data we process and why

  • Your name, email address, time zone, and account ID are used for registration, signing in, and dating your activities. If your account has a password, the server stores its hash, not a password that can be read back.
  • If you sign in with Google, WasteNot uses a short-lived Google ID token to verify your identity. It uses your Google account identifier to recognize you and may use your verified email address and name to set up your WasteNot account. It does not retain Google ID tokens or ask for access to your mail, files, or contacts. Google handles sign-in under the Google privacy policy.
  • Session tokens and their expiry are used to authenticate you and to revoke access. You never need to send your access or refresh tokens to the maintainer.
  • Activity logs contain the waste category, handling method, weight or count, date, text notes, and optional attachments. The server uses them for your history, statistics, XP, and achievements.
  • Request IDs and log versions are used to sync your data, prevent duplicate entries, and resolve conflicting edits.
  • Network requests carry your IP address and technical metadata to the server or service provider they reach. The services use this request information to operate, to limit abuse, and to investigate problems. Operational logs may be kept separately from account data.

Account data is stored in the WasteNot backend's PostgreSQL database. Photo attachments are stored separately in private server storage and are only served through an API that checks who owns them. This is not end-to-end encryption: the server systems and the people who run the infrastructure can process data to keep the service running.

Photos and AI services

When you choose to run a scan, the app sends the photo to the WasteNot backend. The backend normalizes the image and sends it to the WasteNot detection service, which runs an object detection model. This does not turn the scan photo into a log attachment.

The detection flow never stores the raw scan photo as a permanent attachment in the database. The server keeps the AI results and operation metadata, including the object, the condition you confirmed, and the recommendation, so it can continue the session and handle repeated requests. AI operations last about 24 hours and are then cleaned up by a maintenance job. That doesn't guarantee every copy in memory, infrastructure logs, or backups disappears the moment they expire.

Recommendations may use the Google Gemini API. The request to Gemini contains the object type, category, condition, handling options, risk indicators, and the relevant guide evidence. It never includes your photo, name, email, or full account history. If the provider is unavailable, the service can pick a method from the existing evidence rules instead.

Google processes this data under the Gemini API terms and the Google privacy policy, and its data-use terms differ by service type. We don't promise zero retention, no training use, or no human review by the provider without evidence of the service configuration in effect. Don't add personal information to the context when you don't need to.

If you add a photo attachment to a log, the file is uploaded and stored separately so you can view it again from your account. The backend normalizes the image and strips its metadata during upload. Don't rely on metadata removal to hide anything that is visible in the image itself.

AI results are not a safety check or a human review. You still need to check the item's condition and what your local collection facility accepts.

Android storage and permissions

The app keeps copies of your logs, server summaries, account identity, editor drafts, and pending changes in its private storage. The local queue lets changes wait for a connection; a change that is waiting to sync may not have reached the server yet. Not all of this local data is encrypted by the app.

The refresh token is stored in a file encrypted with an Android Keystore key. Theme and reminder preferences are also stored on the device. The app turns off Android backup for its data, but device policies and copies you make yourself are outside WasteNot's control.

Camera photos for scans are kept temporarily in the account's private cache. The app schedules scan photos for deletion after about an hour and may delete them sooner when you close the flow. Android scheduling, file errors, or a stopped process can delay this cleanup. Original photos you pick from your gallery stay in your gallery until you delete them yourself.

  • Camera permission is used to take photos when you open the camera feature. Denying it doesn't stop you from logging manually.
  • The Android photo picker gives the app access only to the photos you choose. WasteNot never asks for permission to read your whole gallery.
  • Notification permission is optional and only used for local reminders. You can turn it off in the app or in Android settings.
  • The list of collection points doesn't need location permission. The Maps button opens an address search in a maps app or browser, and that provider handles the request under its own policy.

This privacy page uses no forms, analytics trackers, cookies, or script-based browser storage. Fonts and icons load from the WasteNot domain. Visiting the page still sends network requests to the site's server. External links and email only open when you choose them.

Deletion and retention

Account data and logs are kept for as long as your account needs them, until you delete them with the features provided. Uninstalling the app or signing out is not a request to delete your account on the server.

  • Deleting a log removes it from your active history and totals. The backend marks deleted logs instead of erasing them, so deleting one log is not a promise that its database row is destroyed right away.
  • Attachments that you detach, delete, or never use may be deactivated first. Cleaning up the physical file is separate from the database change and can be delayed or fail. The expiry of a temporary attachment is not a promise that the file is destroyed on time.
  • To delete your account, open Profile, then Settings, then Delete account. Read the confirmation and verify your identity with your current WasteNot password or, for an account without a password, with Google. Once the server accepts the deletion, your account's data in the active database is deleted and your sessions are revoked. Private photo cleanup runs separately with retries, so files may remain until it succeeds.
  • The app clears your account's local data when the deletion succeeds. If the request fails or your device is offline, don't assume your server account is gone. Follow the app's message or contact the maintainer.

Database backups, operational archives, infrastructure logs, data on your other devices, and data the provider has already processed may be kept for different periods. Deleting data from the active systems doesn't automatically destroy older backups or archives. We haven't yet published a guaranteed time limit for deleting every backup; contact the maintainer for the current details.

If you can't sign in, send a deletion request to the maintainer's email, from your account's address if you can. The maintainer has to verify that the account is yours before acting. Don't send passwords, session tokens, recovery codes, or identity documents unless the maintainer asks for them through a process they explain.

Password recovery by email only works for accounts with a WasteNot password when the server's email delivery is configured and available. If you joined with Google and have no WasteNot password, sign in with the same Google account. An error message from email recovery does not mean an email was sent.
